How ripe is my avocado?

Take a photo of a Hass avocado to see its ripening stage and how many days it has left.

The first scan downloads the image model (about 30 MB) – Wi-Fi recommended. After that it's saved on your phone.

For the best result

  • 1Put the avocado on white paper or a white plate.
  • 2Use daylight or bright, even light. Avoid strong shadows.
  • 3Keep the whole avocado in view, upright, in the middle of the picture.

The five stages

1 Underripe · green, very firm
2 Breaking · olive-brown, slight give
3 Ripe, early · purple spots, slices well
4 Ripe, late · uniformly purple, soft
5 Overripe · past its prime
How it works

The scanner finds the avocado in your photo and looks at it in two ways: a pretrained image network (DINOv2) that picks up shape, wrinkles and spots, and measurements of the skin's colour. A classifier trained on 14,710 lab photos of 478 Hass avocados, photographed every day as they ripened, combines the two.

On avocados it has never seen, it gets the exact stage right – of the time and is within one stage – of the time. It spots – of overripe avocados; the rest it usually calls "ripe, late", because overripeness often doesn't show on the skin. Phone photos in kitchen lighting are harder than lab photos, so treat the result as a guide.

The "days left" come from how long the avocados in the study took to go from each stage to ripe (stage 3) and to overripe (stage 5), either at room temperature or at 10 °C. A household fridge (around 4 °C) is likely to slow ripening even more.

Your photo is analysed on your phone and never uploaded. The image network (about 30 MB) is downloaded the first time you scan and then saved on your phone.

Data: Xavier, P., Rodrigues, P. & Silva, C. L. M. (2024). 'Hass' Avocado Ripening Photographic Dataset. Mendeley Data, V1. doi:10.17632/3xd9n945v8.1. Licensed CC BY 4.0.
